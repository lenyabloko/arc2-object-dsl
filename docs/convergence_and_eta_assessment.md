# Convergence of the nondeterministic Wake–Dream cycle, and realistic ETA

Prepared 2026-09-25. This assessment comes before any code generation. It is based on the governing docs (`VERTICAL_CLOSURE_ALGORITHMS.md` §6 and §13–16, `VERTICAL_CLOSURE_AND_CLASS_DIFFERENTIATION.md` "Convergence and explosion guards", `KNOWLEDGE_MODEL.md` BBB policy, `ASSISTANT_CAPABILITY_GAP_UI_CONTRACT.md`), the recorded outcomes, and `recovery_isolation_manifest.md`. I didn't find Codex's August convergence assessment in the working tree, so this is an independent assessment.

## 1. The cycle being assessed

Online (train time), one cycle *k* maps ontology state `O_k` to `O_{k+1}`:

```
Wake(O_k) on training population
  -> capability gaps G_k (first missing layer per packet, medoid clusters)
  -> LLM proposals P_k (new_hypothesis / new_differentiator)       [nondeterministic]
  -> human accept / reject / perturb in Assistant                   [nondeterministic]
  -> deterministic closure C(A,O) + differentiation + RDR compression
  -> LLM realization, regenerated from perturbed concepts           [nondeterministic]
  -> dependency-scoped Wake replay, casualty gates
  -> O_{k+1}
```

Offline (Kaggle) is the same loop with the three nondeterministic steps removed.

## 2. What converges and what doesn't

| Component | Convergence property | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Deterministic closure within a cycle | **Terminates.** It reaches a unique least fixed point. | Positive, range-restricted Horn rules over a frozen finite universe `U`, with semi-naive saturation (§6). Standard result. |
| Differentiation | **Terminates within a cycle.** | Deterministic partitioning with a positive-gain test (§9). |
| The online cycle as a whole | **No convergence guarantee.** | See 2.1–2.5. |
| Offline cycle | **Converges trivially.** | It can't grow the vocabulary, so it reaches a fixed point relative to `O` immediately (the F4 finding). This is convergence without learning. |

### 2.1 No global potential function

A loop converges when every accepted step strictly improves a quantity that is bounded below and can only change in discrete amounts. The design's lexicographic objective (§16) doesn't meet that:

- **Solved-task count** is bounded (at most N), but it's protected rather than required to increase.
- **Gap count** isn't monotone. Accepting a class creates new obligations (detector-composition obligation, shadow frontier, RDR frontier), so the number of gaps can rise as the ontology grows.
- **Compression gain** is local to a single split (§9), not global over ontology + RDR + realizations.
- **Admission.** A perturbation is admitted when a human accepts it, and it "changes the audited closure signature". Changing the signature counts as movement, not as improvement.

So nothing stops the ontology from growing without limit, or from cycling. The record matches that: the ontology grew to `conceptual_bridge.ttl` (3 MB), `wake_rule_generated_profiles.ttl` (5 MB) and 552 rules, while offline transfer stayed at 0 and hidden transfer at 0.00. The guard "at most five complete convergence transactions" is a cap, not a convergence argument.

### 2.2 The MDL objective is incomplete

MDL is stated as compressing the RDR forest plus maximizing concept subsumption. That covers only the model side, and only part of it. A two-part MDL score, `L(model) + L(data | model)`, gives a proper potential when:

- `L(model)` includes the ontology, the RDR forest and the realizations, the latter measured in GKAT/DSL primitive units (realizations as data, per the BBB fix); and
- `L(data | model)` encodes the unsolved residual of the training population.

If a perturbation is admitted only when it strictly lowers this total, the loop is a descent on a nonnegative score that changes in discrete amounts. It therefore **terminates at a local optimum**, whatever the LLM or the human proposes. This is the one change that turns the cycle from "no guarantee" into "guaranteed to converge". The human and the LLM still steer which local optimum it reaches; they can't make it diverge.

### 2.3 Nondeterministic realization makes the objective itself unstable

When realizations are regenerated by an LLM every cycle, `L(data | model)` can change without any change to `O`. That produces oscillation, apparent casualties and apparent recoveries. Convergence therefore needs:

- realizations keyed to the digest of the concept they come from, regenerated only when that concept changes; and
- the regeneration-stability check: regenerate from scratch and require the same solved set.

### 2.4 The lattice can blow up combinatorially

The "permutation-union over all intersections" abduction, and the concept lattice in general, can grow exponentially in the size of the related detector set. The existing guards (64 exemplars, bounded subclasses, cross-case support) cap how much is materialized, but they don't cap the size of the lattice being searched. The two-part MDL of 2.2 prunes it properly: a concept that doesn't pay for its own description length never gets admitted.

### 2.5 Converging on training data isn't generalization

Even a loop that converges can reach a local optimum that fits the training tasks and doesn't transfer. That's exactly the historical failure. The 22 sealed evaluation tasks (never reached by the LLM Dream phase) should act as early-stopping and validation signals: counted after predictions are frozen, never displayed, and never used for admission. With only 22 tasks, each one is about 4.5%. The signal can tell "no transfer" apart from "some transfer"; it can't resolve differences of a few percent.

### 2.6 Throughput bound: human review

Each online cycle needs human review, and the Assistant is the bottleneck. With a working Assistant, say 20–40 reviewed perturbations per week at one part-time reviewer (an assumption). Without one, the loop falls back to agent-only admission, which is the regime that drifted.

### Verdict

- **As specified, the online nondeterministic cycle has no convergence guarantee.** The deterministic sub-steps terminate; the cycle as a whole can grow without limit or oscillate.
- **It becomes provably convergent to a local optimum** with three changes:
  1. two-part MDL, including realizations in primitive units, as a strict-decrease admission rule;
  2. realizations cached by concept digest and checked for regeneration stability;
  3. sealed-set validation for early stopping.
- **Whether that local optimum is any good is an empirical question.** It depends on whether the primitive set and the ARCGraph abstractions can express ARC-AGI-2 solutions compactly. That hasn't been measured; the current evidence is 2/172 with a 5-primitive fragment.

## 3. External constraints

- **Deadline.** ARC Prize 2026 submissions are due **November 2, 2026**, with results on December 4 ([ARC Prize 2026](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026)). That's about 5.5 weeks away.
- **Base rates on ARC-AGI-2.** Leading ARC-AGI-1 approaches scored 1.6–2.5% on ARC-AGI-2. The 2025 Kaggle leaders reached 24.0 / 16.5 / 12.6% with neural test-time-training systems ([ARC-AGI-2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11831), [ARC Prize 2025 results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis)). No symbolic, ontology-based system has published a meaningful ARC-AGI-2 score.
- **History.** The same architecture, pursued at about 130 commits per week for 10 weeks, produced zero measured offline or hidden transfer. Any estimate has to account for that.
